Phase 3 built networks out of dense layers. Every weight in those layers connects to every input, so they treat an input as a flat vector and they relearn the same feature at every position in an image. Convolutions fix both problems by sharing weights across space.

This phase covers what came after that fix and before transformers took over: vision architectures, reusing pretrained backbones, reading gradients out of a trained model, and recurrent networks for sequences. The last page ends at the fixed-vector bottleneck that motivated attention, which is where Phase 5 starts.

## Prerequisites

- [Neurons and Activations](/ai/neural-networks/neurons-and-activations) for ReLU and its variants, which every conv block uses
- [Normalization](/ai/neural-networks/normalization) for BatchNorm and GroupNorm, which appear in ResNet blocks and in small-batch detection models
- [Matrix Multiplication](/ai/pytorch/matrix-multiplication) for `einsum`, which is how a convolution is written when you want to see what it computes
- [Autograd Engine](/ai/pytorch/autograd) for `requires_grad`, `backward()`, and `no_grad()`, which the whole gradient track runs on

Install the vision extras:

```bash
uv add torchvision timm captum
```

`torchvision` supplies datasets and transforms, `timm` supplies pretrained backbones, and `captum` supplies attribution methods you can compare your own hooks against.

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- Compute the output size of any conv layer from its input size, kernel, stride, padding, and dilation, and state the receptive field of a given depth
- Explain what a residual connection does to the gradient, and why it let networks go from 8 layers to 150
- Freeze a backbone and train only a new head, then say when unfreezing is worth the cost
- Pull a Grad-CAM heatmap out of a model with `register_forward_hook` and a backward hook, without `captum`
- Generate a PGD adversarial example and state why it is stronger than FGSM
- Unroll an LSTM over a sliding window, and explain why its gradient does not vanish the way a vanilla RNN's does
- Describe the Seq2Seq bottleneck precisely enough to explain why attention was invented

## Canonical resources

- [`huggingface/pytorch-image-models`](https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models) (`timm`) — pretrained backbones, augmentations, and training scripts covering most published vision architectures
- [`pytorch/captum`](https://github.com/pytorch/captum) — feature attribution methods including Grad-CAM, integrated gradients, and saliency, useful as a reference implementation
- [`Harry24k/adversarial-attacks-pytorch`](https://github.com/Harry24k/adversarial-attacks-pytorch) — FGSM, PGD, and C&W attacks in a few hundred readable lines
- Stanford CS231n, _Deep Learning for Computer Vision_ — the derivation of backprop through conv layers, receptive fields, and the visual analysis material
- Chris Olah, [_Feature Visualization_](https://distill.pub/2017/feature-visualization) — activation maximization, and what individual units respond to
- _Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition_ (He et al., arXiv:1512.03385) — the residual connection and the 152-layer network it enabled
- _Going Deeper with Convolutions_ (Szegedy et al., arXiv:1409.4842) — Inception and the multi-scale block
- _Visualizing and Understanding Convolutional Networks_ (Zeiler and Fergus, arXiv:1311.2901) — deconvnets, the origin of most feature-map visualization
- _Long Short-Term Memory_ (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997) — the gated cell and the constant error carousel
- _Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition_ (LeCun et al., 1998) — LeNet, and the first statement of why weight sharing beats dense layers on images
- _Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases_ (Mikolov et al., arXiv:1310.4546) — Word2Vec and negative sampling
- _Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks_ (Sutskever et al., arXiv:1409.3215) — the encoder-decoder that created the bottleneck problem
- _Deep Visual-Semantic Alignments for Generating Image Descriptions_ (Karpathy and Fei-Fei, arXiv:1412.2306) — CNN-to-RNN captioning
- _A Neural Algorithm of Artistic Style_ (Gatys et al., arXiv:1508.06576) — the Gram-matrix style loss and L-BFGS optimization
