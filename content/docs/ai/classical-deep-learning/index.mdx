Phase 3 built networks out of dense layers. Every weight in those layers connects to every input, so they treat an input as a flat vector and relearn the same feature at every position. That is wasteful on the two kinds of data most of AI is about: images, where nearby pixels belong together, and sequences, where order matters and length varies.

This phase covers the architectures built for those two kinds of data before transformers took over: convolutional networks for images, and recurrent networks for sequences. It ends at the exact weakness of recurrent encoder-decoders that attention was invented to fix, which is where Phase 5 starts.

Every page is written to be read in order within its track. Each one states what it builds on, defines each term at the point it is first used, works at least one example by hand with real numbers, and closes with a table of key terms and a set of interview questions.

- **Recurrent networks** — Recurrent Neural Networks, Vanishing and Exploding Gradients, LSTM and GRU. Each page answers the question the previous one raises: what an RNN is, why it forgets, how gates fix it.

## The whole phase, topic by topic

Every page in this phase, in reading order, with the one detail that makes each section worth remembering. Read it first as a preview, or afterwards as a revision sheet.

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="Vision and Sequence Basics">

    - **Why dense layers are not enough** — about 9.6 million position-tied weights for one small image layer; no way to handle variable length or order
    - **Images as tensors** — pixels, channels, `(B, C, H, W)`, scaling to 0..1, and per-channel normalization
    - **Computer vision tasks and datasets** — classification, detection, segmentation, captioning; MNIST, CIFAR-10, ImageNet
    - **Sequences as tensors** — `(B, T, F)`; text, audio, and time series; what NLP is
    - **Inductive bias** — locality and translation equivariance for convolutions, order for recurrence
    - **The three families** — CNNs for space, RNNs for time, transformers for both
    - **Pretrain, then adapt** — backbone, head, transfer learning, fine-tuning

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Convolutions">

    - **Why images need a different layer** — locality and weight sharing: 1,792 parameters instead of 9.6 million
    - **The operation, computed by hand** — a vertical-edge kernel produces 0 on flat regions and 3 on the edge
    - **Filters are learned** — a shared kernel's gradient is summed over every position
    - **Channels** — a filter spans all input channels; one filter makes one feature map
    - **Padding, stride, dilation** — then the output size formula $\lfloor (H + 2p - d(k-1) - 1)/s \rfloor + 1$
    - **Pooling** — max over windows; global average pooling ends a classifier
    - **The feature hierarchy and receptive fields** — edges, textures, parts, objects; why two $3 \times 3$ beat one $5 \times 5$
    - **Efficient variants** — $1 \times 1$, grouped, depthwise separable, im2col

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="CNN Architectures">

    - **LeNet-5** — the convolution, pooling, classifier template
    - **AlexNet** — ReLU, GPUs, dropout, augmentation; 15.3% against 26.2%
    - **VGG** — depth from uniform $3 \times 3$ stacks; 124M of 138M parameters in the dense head
    - **Inception** — parallel kernel sizes; a $1 \times 1$ reduction makes a branch twelve times cheaper
    - **ResNet** — the degradation problem is an optimization failure; $\mathbf{y} = F(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{x}$ gives an identity gradient path
    - **After ResNet** — DenseNet, MobileNet, SENet, EfficientNet, ViT, ConvNeXt
    - **Training in practice** — label-preserving augmentation, MixUp and CutMix, a CIFAR-10 recipe, timm backbones

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Transfer Learning and Ensembles">

    - **Transfer learning** — linear probing, fine-tuning, and how the domain gap decides between them
    - **Ensembles** — logit versus probability averaging, bagging, and evaluating many models at once with `vmap`

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Video Models">

    - **Clips as tensors** — `(B, C, T, H, W)`
    - **3D and (2+1)D convolutions** — factorizing space and time is cheaper and more accurate
    - **Temporal sampling** — the frame interval sets which motions can be seen

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Interpretability">

    - **Hooks** — callbacks on a layer's forward or backward pass
    - **Inspecting feature maps** — and finding what a unit responds to from its top-activating images
    - **Saliency maps** — the gradient of a class score with respect to each pixel
    - **Grad-CAM** — feature maps weighted by their gradient; Guided Grad-CAM adds pixel detail
    - **Attribution methods and captum** — integrated gradients and other ready-made methods

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Adversarial Examples and Style Transfer">

    - **Adversarial examples** — invisible perturbations that flip predictions, explained by near-linearity in high dimensions
    - **FGSM and PGD** — one signed gradient step, or many projected ones
    - **Adversarial training** — training on perturbed examples, the only defense that has held up
    - **Deep Dream and style transfer** — gradient ascent on activations; content from deep features, style from Gram matrices

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Recurrent Neural Networks">

    - **What makes sequences different** — order, variable length, long-range relations; why dense, window, and bag-of-words models fail
    - **The core idea** — $\mathbf{h}_t = f_\theta(\mathbf{h}_{t-1}, \mathbf{x}_t)$ with the same weights at every step
    - **The vanilla cell, worked by hand** — a single event fades 0.76, 0.36, 0.18, 0.09
    - **Unrolling and BPTT** — depth along time equals sequence length
    - **Task patterns** — many-to-one, one-to-many, aligned and unaligned many-to-many
    - **A character language model** — why `hello` needs memory at the two `l`s
    - **Stacked and bidirectional RNNs**, and the PyTorch shapes

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Vanishing and Exploding Gradients">

    - **Long-range dependencies** — `keys … are`
    - **The scalar case** — a per-step factor of 0.72 leaves $7 \times 10^{-8}$ of the gradient after 50 steps
    - **The vector case** — a product of Jacobians bounded by $(\gamma\rho)^{T-k}$
    - **Why the failure is silent** — short-range terms dominate the summed gradient
    - **Clipping** — fixes explosion only; norm clipping keeps direction
    - **Truncated BPTT** — memory $O(L)$, no dependency longer than $L$ is learned
    - **Partial remedies** — and why the fix had to be architectural

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="LSTM and GRU">

    - **The design goal** — memory that passes unchanged by default, changed only by explicit erase and write
    - **What a gate is** — a sigmoid vector applied with $\odot$
    - **The cell, step by step** — forget, candidate and input, additive update, output
    - **Worked example** — a "plural subject" value stored at `keys`, held, and exposed at `are`
    - **Why the gradient survives** — $\partial\mathbf{c}_t / \partial\mathbf{c}_{t-1} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{f}_t)$; 0.98 over 100 steps is 0.13, not $10^{-15}$
    - **GRU** — update and reset gates; what tying input to $1 -$ forget gives up
    - **Using them** — classification, forecasting, and where recurrence still wins

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Classical NLP: Text as Numbers">

    - **Tokens and vocabularies** — word, character, subword; `<unk>`, `<pad>`, `<bos>`, `<eos>`
    - **One-hot vectors** — lossless but every pair of words equally different
    - **Bag of words and n-grams** — cosine similarity between documents
    - **TF-IDF** — `the` scores 0, `mat` scores 1.099
    - **N-gram language models** — the Markov assumption, sparsity, smoothing
    - **Perplexity** — the effective number of choices per token
    - **What counting cannot do** — the distributional hypothesis

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Word Embeddings">

    - **Dense vectors with meaning** — similarity is geometric, knowledge transfers
    - **Co-occurrence matrices and PMI** — count-based vectors and SVD
    - **The embedding layer** — a one-hot times a matrix, implemented as a lookup
    - **Word2Vec** — skip-gram pairs, two tables, negative sampling worked by hand, the $3/4$ power, subsampling
    - **GloVe** — the ice/steam ratios, $\mathbf{w}_i^\top\tilde{\mathbf{w}}_j + b_i + \tilde{b}_j \approx \log X_{ij}$, the weighting function
    - **fastText** — vectors from character n-grams
    - **Limits** — polysemy, bias, and the move to contextual embeddings

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Seq2Seq and Attention">

    - **The encoder-decoder** — a context vector and a conditional language model
    - **Teacher forcing** — and exposure bias
    - **Greedy versus beam search** — `a cat` at 0.36 beats greedy's 0.20
    - **The fixed-vector bottleneck** — BLEU falls off past about 20 words
    - **Attention** — scores, softmax weights, a weighted sum; worked with three 2-D states
    - **The road to the transformer** — queries, keys, values, and dropping the recurrence
    - **Captioning and BLEU** — a CNN encoder with attention over regions; BLEU worked by hand

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Graph Neural Networks">

    - **Why graphs need their own architecture** — node order is arbitrary; permutation equivariance
    - **Message passing** — message, aggregate, update; GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT, GIN
    - **The GCN layer by hand** — a signal spreading one hop per layer around a 4-cycle
    - **Over-smoothing, over-squashing, and the 1-WL ceiling** — why GNNs stay shallow

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Generative Models">

    - **Learning a distribution** — the normalizer problem and three workarounds
    - **Autoencoders and VAEs** — the ELBO, the reparameterization trick, and blurry samples
    - **GANs** — the minimax game, $D^*$, the non-saturating loss, mode collapse
    - **Diffusion** — closed-form noising, noise-prediction MSE, fast samplers, latent diffusion, guidance

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Architecture Map">

    - **The map** — six backbones and three generative frameworks, as two independent axes
    - **Choosing by data structure** — each backbone is a dense layer with a constraint added
    - **Practical defaults** — trees for tabular data, pretrained transformers for text, diffusion for generation

  </Accordion>
</Accordions>

## Prerequisites

- [Neurons and Activations](/ai/neural-networks/neurons-and-activations) for ReLU, `tanh`, and the sigmoid, which every conv block and every recurrent gate uses
- [Normalization](/ai/neural-networks/normalization) for BatchNorm, which appears in every modern CNN block
- [Backprop from Scratch](/ai/math-foundations/backprop-from-scratch) for the chain rule, which the vanishing-gradient derivation applies step by step
- [Similarity and Norms](/ai/math-foundations/similarity-and-norms) for the dot product and cosine similarity used by embeddings and attention
- [Autograd Engine](/ai/pytorch/autograd-engine) for `requires_grad`, `backward()`, and `no_grad()`, which the interpretability track runs on

Install the vision extras:

uv add torchvision timm captum

## What should be possible afterwards

- Compute a convolution by hand, the output size of any conv layer, and the receptive field at a given depth
- Explain the degradation problem and why a residual connection fixes it, in terms of both the function and the gradient
- Freeze a backbone and train only a new head, and say when unfreezing is worth the cost
- Pull a Grad-CAM heatmap out of a model with hooks, and generate a PGD adversarial example
- Define an RNN, run its recurrence by hand, and derive why its gradient vanishes over long distances
- Walk through every LSTM equation, and point at the term that keeps its gradient alive
- Compute TF-IDF and perplexity by hand, and explain how Word2Vec and GloVe learn vectors from co-occurrence
- Compute an attention context vector by hand, and explain the Seq2Seq bottleneck precisely enough to say why attention was invented

## Canonical resources

- Stanford CS231n, _Deep Learning for Computer Vision_ — convolutions, receptive fields, and CNN architectures
- Stanford CS224n, _Natural Language Processing with Deep Learning_ — word vectors, RNNs, LSTMs, Seq2Seq, and attention, in the same order as this phase
- Christopher Olah, [_Understanding LSTM Networks_](https://colah.github.io/posts/2015-08-Understanding-LSTMs/) — the clearest diagrams of the LSTM cell
- Andrej Karpathy, [_The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Recurrent Neural Networks_](https://karpathy.github.io/2015/05/21/rnn-effectiveness/) — char-RNN and what its hidden units learn
- [`huggingface/pytorch-image-models`](https://github.com/huggingface/pytorch-image-models) (`timm`) — pretrained backbones and training scripts for most published vision architectures
- [`pytorch/captum`](https://github.com/pytorch/captum) — reference implementations of Grad-CAM, integrated gradients, and saliency
- _Gradient-Based Learning Applied to Document Recognition_ (LeCun et al., 1998) — LeNet, and why weight sharing beats dense layers on images
- _ImageNet Classification with Deep Convolutional Neural Networks_ (Krizhevsky et al., 2012) — AlexNet
- _Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition_ (He et al., arXiv:1512.03385) — the degradation problem and the residual connection
- _Going Deeper with Convolutions_ (Szegedy et al., arXiv:1409.4842) — Inception and the multi-scale block
- _On the Difficulty of Training Recurrent Neural Networks_ (Pascanu et al., arXiv:1211.5063) — the vanishing and exploding gradient analysis and norm clipping
- _Long Short-Term Memory_ (Hochreiter and Schmidhuber, 1997) — the gated cell and the constant error carousel
- _Learning Phrase Representations using RNN Encoder-Decoder_ (Cho et al., arXiv:1406.1078) — the GRU and the encoder-decoder
- _Distributed Representations of Words and Phrases_ (Mikolov et al., arXiv:1310.4546) — Word2Vec and negative sampling
- _GloVe: Global Vectors for Word Representation_ (Pennington et al., 2014) — the co-occurrence ratio argument and the weighted least-squares objective
- _Sequence to Sequence Learning with Neural Networks_ (Sutskever et al., arXiv:1409.3215) — the encoder-decoder that created the bottleneck
- _Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate_ (Bahdanau et al., arXiv:1409.0473) — attention
- _A Neural Algorithm of Artistic Style_ (Gatys et al., arXiv:1508.06576) — the Gram-matrix style loss
