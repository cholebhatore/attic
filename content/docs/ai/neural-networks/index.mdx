The earlier sections supplied the mathematics (vectors, derivatives, probability) and the machinery (PyTorch tensors, autograd, a training loop). This section builds neural networks themselves, from a single neuron to a deep network that trains reliably and generalizes to new data.

Every network, however large, is the same pattern repeated: a layer mixes its inputs with a matrix multiply, bends the result with a nonlinear activation, and hands it to the next layer. Training adjusts the matrices by gradient descent. The pages are ordered so that each one answers the question the previous one leaves open:

1. **What is computed?** A neuron, a layer, a network.
2. **How does it learn?** A loss to measure error, gradient descent to reduce it, backpropagation to compute the gradients.
3. **Why does it stop working when deep, and how is that fixed?** Initialization and normalization keep signals at a usable scale.
4. **How can each step be better?** Optimizers and learning-rate schedules.
5. **How does it work on new data?** Regularization, and choosing hyperparameters.
6. **How is it done in practice?** A training and debugging procedure.

Each page defines its terms where they first appear, works at least one example by hand with real numbers, and ends with a table of key terms and interview questions.

## The whole section, topic by topic

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="What Is a Neural Network">

    - **The perceptron and XOR** — one neuron draws one straight line; two hidden ReLU units solve XOR exactly
    - **Anatomy** — input, hidden, and output layers; width and depth
    - **Counting parameters** — a 784-128-10 network has 101,770
    - **Universal approximation** — possible is not the same as trainable

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Neurons and Activations">

    - **One neuron** — $z = \mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{x} + b$, then $a = f(z)$; a decision boundary
    - **A layer** — $\mathbf{W}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}$, one matrix multiply for a whole batch
    - **Why activations matter** — two linear layers collapse into one
    - **Sigmoid and tanh** — saturation: $\tanh'(3) = 0.0099$
    - **ReLU** — derivative 1 when active; dead units; Leaky ReLU and ELU
    - **GELU, SiLU, SwiGLU** — smooth gates, used in transformers
    - **Output activations** — identity, sigmoid, softmax, matched to the task

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Loss Functions">

    - **Loss versus metric** — accuracy has zero gradient almost everywhere
    - **MSE, MAE, Huber** — one outlier multiplies MSE by 55 and MAE by 5
    - **Cross-entropy** — the gradient with respect to the logits is $\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{y}$
    - **Initial loss** — about $\ln C$ for an untrained classifier
    - **Logits and stability**, **class imbalance**, **choosing a loss**

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Gradient Descent">

    - **The update** — $\boldsymbol\theta \leftarrow \boldsymbol\theta - \eta\nabla L$
    - **One dimension by hand** — learning rates 0.1, 0.5, 0.9, 1.1: converge, jump, oscillate, diverge
    - **Fitting a line by hand** — loss 18.67 to 3.76 in one step
    - **Mini-batches** — unbiased, cheap, and noisy in a useful way; epochs and iterations
    - **Landscapes** — saddle points outnumber local minima

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Backpropagation">

    - **Two neurons by hand** — upstream gradient times local gradient
    - **Reverse mode** — one output, millions of inputs
    - **Four local rules** — matmul, bias, element-wise, softmax cross-entropy
    - **An MLP in NumPy** — seven gradients, 99% accuracy
    - **Gradient checking**, **the shape contract**, **the missing 1/N bug**

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Weight Initialization">

    - **Symmetry** — equal weights stay equal forever
    - **Variance through a layer** — $\text{Var}(y) = n\,\text{Var}(w)\,\text{Var}(x)$
    - **Gains multiply** — 0.5 per layer is $10^{-12}$ after 40
    - **Xavier and He** — $2/(n_{\text{in}} + n_{\text{out}})$ and $2/n_{\text{in}}$
    - **PyTorch's default** — $1/(3n)$, and why it usually works anyway

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Normalization">

    - **By hand** — $[1, 2, 3] \to [-1.225, 0, 1.225]$, then $\gamma$ and $\beta$
    - **The one choice** — which values are averaged together
    - **BatchNorm** — running statistics, train versus eval, and the batch-size problem
    - **LayerNorm, RMSNorm, GroupNorm** — per-sample alternatives
    - **Pre-norm versus post-norm** — a clean residual path

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Optimizers">

    - **SGD's two problems** — ill-conditioning and differing gradient scales
    - **Momentum** — consistent gradients amplified tenfold, oscillating ones halved
    - **RMSprop and Adam** — per-parameter scaling; bias correction turns a $3.16\eta$ first step into $\eta$
    - **AdamW** — L2 under Adam can have no effect at all
    - **Lion, Adafactor, memory cost** — AdamW needs 16 bytes per parameter

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Learning Rate Schedules">

    - **Two jobs** — warm up at the start, anneal at the end
    - **A schedule by hand** — cosine is exactly half the peak at its midpoint
    - **Decay shapes** — step, exponential, cosine, linear, inverse square root
    - **WSD and SGDR** — open-ended budgets and restarts
    - **Peak rate matters more than shape**

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Overfitting and Regularization">

    - **Three splits** — training, validation, test, and leakage
    - **Polynomials** — degree 9 gets training error 0.004 and validation error 0.88
    - **Bias and variance**, **learning curves**, **double descent**
    - **Weight decay** — $\lambda = 10^{-4}$ cuts validation error 14×
    - **Dropout, early stopping, label smoothing**

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Hyperparameter Search">

    - **What matters** — learning rate first
    - **Random beats grid** — 125 learning rates instead of 5 for the same budget
    - **Log-uniform sampling**, **successive halving**, **Hyperband and ASHA**
    - **TPE and Gaussian processes**, **population-based training**
    - **A protocol** — finish by re-running the winners across seeds

  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Training and Debugging">

    - **The recipe** — data, baselines, simple pipeline, fit, regularize, tune
    - **Checks** — initial loss near $\ln C$; overfit one batch; inspect the actual inputs
    - **Loss curves and monitoring** — gradient norm, update-to-weight ratio near $10^{-3}$
    - **Silent bugs and NaN debugging**, **reproducibility**

  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
