## Phase 1: Mathematical foundations

`content/docs/ai/math-foundations`

Build geometric and analytical intuition for high-dimensional vector spaces and optimization. The phase overview opens with the four-step training loop that every page in it belongs to.

**The loop every page belongs to**

- Represent: inputs become vectors, matrices transform them, dot products and norms compare them
- Predict: broadcasting combines arrays of different shapes, softmax turns raw scores into probabilities
- Measure: cross-entropy puts a single number on how wrong those probabilities were
- Update: the gradient says which way to turn each weight, backpropagation computes a million of them

**Reading the math**

- Notation and symbols: shape tuples and axis indexing, what `@`, `.T`, `||v||`, `sum over i of` mean, the Greek letters and size letters used across the phase, and the vocabulary words that carry weight

**Linear algebra and vector spaces**

- Vectors: unit vectors, basis vectors, dimensionality, geometric representation
- Matrices: linear transformations, rank, invertibility, SVD, eigen-decomposition
- Dot product similarity: projections, inner products, cosine similarity, term frequency vectors as the worked example, and why unit-normalized vectors reduce cosine similarity to a plain dot product
- L1 vs L2 norms: Manhattan vs Euclidean distance, sparsity incentives (Lasso), weight decay (Ridge)

**Multivariate calculus and optimization**

- Derivatives: instantaneous rates of change, partial derivatives
- Derivation rules: chain, product, and quotient rules across nested composite functions
- Gradients: Jacobian vectors, directional derivatives, steepness across a multidimensional loss surface, local minima vs saddle points

**Probabilistic and array mechanics**

- Tensor broadcasting: implicit dimension expansion, stride tricks, memory-efficient vectorization
- Softmax and logits `INTERVIEW`: how raw unnormalized scores map to probability distributions, temperature scaling, and numerical stability through the log-sum-exp trick
- Information theory and loss functions: entropy, cross-entropy loss, KL divergence, perplexity

**Algorithmic implementation**

- Backprop in Python: construct a directed acyclic computational graph and reverse-mode automatic differentiation from scratch in NumPy

**Canonical resources**

- [`karpathy/micrograd`](https://github.com/karpathy/micrograd) — scalar-valued autograd engine and neural net library
- 3Blue1Brown, _Essence of Linear Algebra_ and _Essence of Calculus_
- Andrej Karpathy, _The spelled-out intro to neural networks and backpropagation: building micrograd_
- Outcome School, _Understanding Logits in Machine Learning_ and _Embeddings in Machine Learning_
- [_Mathematics for Machine Learning_](https://mml-book.github.io) (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong), free PDF

## Phase 3: Neural networks from scratch

`content/docs/ai/neural-networks`

Strip away framework abstractions and build forward passes, normalization layers, and adaptive optimizers from raw math. The phase overview opens with the chain every page in it belongs to.

**The chain every page belongs to**

- Repeat three lines: mix with a matmul, bend with a non-linearity, hand the result to the next layer
- What depth breaks first is the scale of the starting weights, then the size of the gradient arriving back at the front
- Normalization repairs the conditioning, and in doing so changes which initialization you need and how small a batch you may use
- Learning rate, optimizer, and search budget decide whether the construction ever learns anything

**Architecture primitives**

- Single neuron from scratch: affine transforms (`z = Wx + b`) and non-linear activations (`ReLU`, `GELU`, `SiLU`/`SwiGLU`)
- Building a layer: vectorized dense layers and variance-preserving initialization (Xavier/Glorot, He/Kaiming)
- Implementing a network: multi-layer perceptrons, caching forward activations, computing analytical backward gradients, and checking them against a central finite difference

**Training dynamics, normalization, and optimizers**

- BatchNorm vs LayerNorm: internal covariate shift and why LayerNorm wins in sequence models
- RMSNorm: dropping mean-centering for speed in modern LLMs, plus GroupNorm and pre-norm against post-norm
- Learning rate and decay: linear warmup, step decay, cosine annealing, Noam/inverse-sqrt, warmup-stable-decay, and SGDR restarts
- Adam and AdamW: first and second moment estimation, decoupled weight decay, and how Lion, Adafactor, and per-parameter memory cost compare
- Hyper-parameter optimization: grid search, random search, log-uniform spaces, successive halving with Hyperband and ASHA, model-based search with Optuna or Ray Tune, and population based training

**Canonical resources**

- [`karpathy/makemore`](https://github.com/karpathy/makemore)
- [`optuna/optuna`](https://github.com/optuna/optuna)
- Andrej Karpathy, _Neural Networks: Zero to Hero_ (parts 2 to 4)
- _Root Mean Square Layer Normalization_ (Zhang and Sennrich, arXiv:1910.07467)
- _Decoupled Weight Decay Regularization_ (Loshchilov and Hutter, arXiv:1711.05101)

## Phase 5: The transformer architecture

`content/docs/ai/transformers`

Master self-attention internals, forward-pass mechanics, and encoder vs decoder paradigms.

**Attention and transformer internals** `INTERVIEW`

- Attention mechanism: overcoming the Seq2Seq bottleneck, geometric intuition for query, key, and value projections
- Self-attention from scratch: implementing `softmax(Q @ K.T / sqrt(d_k)) @ V` and proving why scaling by `1 / sqrt(d_k)` stops dot products from growing large enough to push softmax into regions with vanishing gradients

- Encoder-only (BERT) vs decoder-only (GPT) vs encoder-decoder (T5) `INTERVIEW`: masked language modeling vs causal language modeling, and why decoder-only models dominate production scaling
- Step-by-step decoder forward pass `INTERVIEW`: token IDs, embedding lookup plus positional encoding, `N` blocks of [pre-RMSNorm, causal MHA, residual, pre-RMSNorm, FFN, residual], final norm, LM head projection, logits

## Phase 7: Post-training, fine-tuning, and distillation

`content/docs/ai/fine-tuning`

Adapt base models with parameter-efficient fine-tuning or compress capabilities through knowledge distillation.

**Strategic decision framework** `INTERVIEW`

- Why fine-tune: prompting and RAG for dynamic external facts, fine-tuning for behavioral alignment, strict formatting, latency reduction, or domain style, plus mitigating catastrophic forgetting
- Hugging Face ecosystem: `transformers`, `datasets`, `accelerate`, `peft`, `trl`, `unsloth`
- Data preparation: high-signal instruction pairs, ChatML templates, multi-turn formatting, synthetic data distillation, decontamination

**Parameter-efficient fine-tuning** `INTERVIEW`

- LoRA: freeze base weights `W0`, inject trainable rank-decomposition matrices where `dW = B @ A`, then choose rank `r`, scaling `alpha`, and target modules
- QLoRA: 4-bit NormalFloat (`NF4`), double quantization, paged optimizers, single-GPU fine-tuning
- DoRA: decomposing pre-trained weights into magnitude and direction to recover more of full fine-tuning quality
- Prefix tuning vs prompt tuning `INTERVIEW`: learnable virtual tokens injected into every layer's key and value states vs virtual tokens added only at the input embedding layer, including exact trainable parameter counts

**Knowledge distillation** `INTERVIEW`

- Teacher-student distillation: transferring capability from a large teacher to a compact student
- Dark knowledge and soft labels: raising temperature (`T > 1`) so the student learns inter-token probability relationships through a KL divergence distillation loss combined with hard-label cross-entropy

**Execution, training, and deployment**

- Training and hyperparameters: LR schedules, gradient accumulation, sequence packing, `bf16` mixed precision, FlashAttention-2
- Real use case capstone: medical scribe, SQL generator, or low-latency classifier
- Evaluation and deployment: merging LoRA adapters into base weights, regression testing, exporting to production serving engines

**Canonical resources**

- [`huggingface/peft`](https://github.com/huggingface/peft) and [`huggingface/alignment-handbook`](https://github.com/huggingface/alignment-handbook)

## Phase 9: Context engineering, vector databases, and RAG

`content/docs/ai/rag`

The most heavily tested AI engineering interview domain: designing, scaling, and debugging production retrieval and context pipelines.

**Prompting, context engineering, and compaction** `INTERVIEW`

- Prompt engineering vs context engineering: tuning the inner box (wording, few-shot examples, chain-of-thought, JSON schema enforcement) vs architecting the outer box (system instructions, retrieved chunks, tool definitions, memory, conversation history, user query)
- Prompt chaining: splitting monolithic prompts into sequential modular steps and preventing cascading error propagation
- Context budgeting and compaction: ordering context from most stable (system prompt, tools) to most fresh (history, query) to maximize prompt caching hits, fighting the lost-in-the-middle problem by placing critical instructions at the very start and very end, recursive summarization, token pruning
- RAG vs long context windows: retrieval vs stuffing a 1M-token window, judged on cost per query, time-to-first-token, and needle-in-a-haystack degradation

**Chunking, embeddings, and vector databases** `INTERVIEW`

- Chunking strategies: fixed-size, recursive character, semantic chunking, and parent-child chunking (retrieve granular child chunks for precision, pass the broader parent chunk to the LLM for context)
- Contextual retrieval: prepending an LLM-generated chunk-specific header before embedding and BM25 indexing so isolated chunks keep document-level context
- Vector DB and ANN search: HNSW graphs, IVF clustering, product quantization, and pre vs post metadata filtering

**Hybrid search, reranking, and query transformation** `INTERVIEW`

- Hybrid search: dense semantic search combined with sparse lexical search (BM25 for exact keyword and jargon matches) through Reciprocal Rank Fusion
- Reranking: retrieval optimizes recall, reranking optimizes precision. Compare cross-encoders, LLM-based rerankers, and ColBERT late interaction, where queries and documents encode into per-token matrices scored by summed maximum cosine similarity:

```text
S(q, d) = sum over i in q of ( max over j in d of dot(E_q[i], E_d[j]) )
```

- Query transformation: HyDE (hypothetical document embeddings), query decomposition into sub-queries, step-back prompting, multi-query retrieval

**Advanced RAG paradigms** `INTERVIEW`

- Self-RAG and agentic RAG: the agent decides whether to retrieve, which source to query, evaluates retrieved relevance, and rewrites queries across multi-hop steps
- GraphRAG: knowledge graphs (entities, relations, hierarchical community summaries) combined with vector search for multi-hop relational and global questions, local vs global search, indexing cost trade-offs
- Vectorless RAG and structured data: routing natural language over SQL (text-to-SQL), markdown tables, and multi-column PDFs

**Production debugging and failure modes** `INTERVIEW`

- Hallucinating despite correct context: strict grounding prompts, lower temperature, structured citation extraction, context pruning
- Redundant or duplicate chunks: chunk overlap tuning, Maximal Marginal Relevance, semantic deduplication
- Slow retrieval at million-document scale: sharding, HNSW parameter tuning (`efSearch`, `M`), scalar and binary embedding quantization, semantic caching
- Per-user access control: enforcing document-level permissions inside metadata filters before vector retrieval so unauthorized chunks never reach the LLM context
- Document updates and freshness: incremental indexing by document hash, TTL invalidation, citation and source attribution

## Phase 10: Inference, quantization, CUDA, and serving

`content/docs/ai/inference`

Optimize GPU execution, compress model weights, and serve LLMs with minimal time-to-first-token and maximum throughput.

**Hardware acceleration and CUDA kernels**

- CUDA fundamentals: the execution hierarchy (threads, warps, thread blocks, streaming multiprocessors), HBM vs on-chip SRAM, memory bandwidth bottlenecks, kernel fusion
- Custom kernels and LPUs: writing GPU kernels in CUDA or OpenAI Triton, and specialized inference silicon like Language Processing Units built for deterministic SRAM token streaming

**Quantization** `INTERVIEW`

- Quantization math: mapping continuous floating-point ranges to discrete integers (`INT8`, `INT4`) using scale and zero-point

```text
q = round(r / scale) + zero_point
```

- Granularity and schemes: per-tensor vs per-channel quantization (preserving accuracy against outlier weights), weight-only (`W4A16`) vs weight-and-activation (`W8A8`)
- PTQ vs QAT: post-training quantization (GPTQ, AWQ protecting salient activation channels, bitsandbytes) vs quantization-aware training that simulates rounding noise during training
- Local inference and edge formats: how Ollama, `llama.cpp`, GGML, and GGUF quantize and run models across GPU, CPU, and Apple Silicon unified memory

**KV cache and latency mechanics** `INTERVIEW`

- The first-token latency problem: prefill is compute-bound and parallel over the whole prompt, decode is memory-bandwidth-bound and sequential, so the first token costs far more than the ones after it
- KV cache estimation and compression:

```text
kv_bytes = 2 * layers * kv_heads * head_dim * precision_bytes * seq_len * batch_size
```

plus KV cache quantization (`FP8`, `INT4`), token eviction, and GQA or MLA reductions

**Speculative decoding and serving engines** `INTERVIEW`

- Speculative decoding variants: draft-and-verify, n-gram speculation (copying repeating n-grams from context), Medusa (multiple decoding heads predicting future tokens verified in parallel through tree attention), EAGLE (autoregressive drafting at the feature level rather than the token level)
- Continuous batching and prompt caching: iteration-level request scheduling that removes padding waste, and reusing computed KV states across shared prompt prefixes
- Prefill-decode disaggregation: separating compute-heavy prefill nodes from bandwidth-heavy decode nodes in production clusters
- Serving engines: vLLM with PagedAttention (KV cache in non-contiguous virtual memory blocks to remove fragmentation), SGLang with RadixAttention (a radix tree of KV caches across requests for automatic multi-turn and agentic prefix reuse) plus constrained structured output decoding, and TensorRT-LLM with C++/CUDA graph compilation and fused kernels

## Phase 11: Agents, MCP, evaluation, and guardrails

`content/docs/ai/agents`

Architect reliable tool-using agents, standardize context connectors with MCP, and implement automated evaluation.

**Agents from first principles** `INTERVIEW`

The five components of an AI agent:

1. **The brain (LLM)**: the reasoning engine that recommends actions or returns final answers
2. **The instructions (system prompt)**: role, constraints, and agent skills (modular folders where only a one-line description stays in context and full procedures load on demand)
3. **The hands (tools)**: external actions defined by `name`, `description` (the routing signal the LLM reads), and a strict JSON `input_schema`
4. **The memory**: short-term (in-flight session trajectory and observations) vs long-term (episodic, semantic, and procedural facts persisted in external vector or relational stores)
5. **The loop (runtime and loop engineering)**: the `Think -> Act -> Observe -> Repeat` controller, step budgets, stopping conditions, and fixing infinite loops with `max_iterations`, stuck-action detectors, and explicit `FINISH` tool calls

Reasoning patterns: ReAct (reason plus act), Plan-and-Execute, Reflexion (self-critique).

**Model Context Protocol and tooling** `INTERVIEW`

- Function calling vs MCP: function calling is how the model asks to invoke a tool, MCP is the open protocol for how host applications discover and connect to tools and data sources
- The MCP triad: host (the AI app such as Claude Desktop or Cursor), client (a 1-to-1 protocol connector inside the host), server (a lightweight process exposing tools, resources, and prompts)

**Agent frameworks, patterns, and security** `INTERVIEW`

- LangChain vs LangGraph: composable pipe chains (`Prompt | LLM | Parser`) vs cyclical stateful graphs with persistence, checkpointing, and human-in-the-loop approvals
- LangChain in practice: seven lessons from one chat-model call to a deployed FastAPI service, at [Agent Frameworks → LangChain](/ai/agents/agent-frameworks/langchain)
- Multi-agent topologies: supervisor and orchestrator-worker patterns, handoffs, parallel sub-agents, deterministic routing
- Agent security and reliability: defending against direct and indirect prompt injection (malicious instructions hidden in retrieved web pages or emails), least-privilege tool scoping, human confirmation gates for destructive actions, sandboxed code execution

**Evals, tracing, and guardrails** `INTERVIEW`

The four evaluation pillars:

1. **Automatic reference metrics**: `BLEU`, `ROUGE`, `BERTScore`, perplexity, exact match
2. **Standard benchmarks**: MMLU, GSM8K, HumanEval, SWE-bench
3. **Human evaluation**: Likert ratings, pairwise comparison (Chatbot Arena, Elo), granular error annotation
4. **LLM-as-a-judge**: rubric-based grading plus mitigation for position bias (swap-order evaluation), verbosity bias (length normalization), and self-preference bias

RAG and agent evaluation: the RAG triad (context precision, context recall, faithfulness or groundedness, answer relevance) with `RAGAS` and `DeepEval`; agent tool selection accuracy, argument validity, step efficiency, task completion.

Observability and guardrails: distributed trace instrumentation (LangSmith, Langfuse, Arize Phoenix) and runtime input/output guardrails (PII redaction, toxicity filtering, hallucination grounding checks).
