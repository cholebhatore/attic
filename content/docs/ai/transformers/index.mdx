Phase 4 ended at a specific failure: a Seq2Seq model compresses an entire source sentence into one fixed vector, and translation quality collapses past about twenty words. Bahdanau's fix was to let the decoder look back at every encoder state and take a weighted sum. That weighted sum, stripped of the recurrence around it, is the transformer.

This phase builds the architecture piece by piece and ends with a complete autoregressive model you can train. Everything in Phases 6 through 12 assumes you can write a transformer block from memory and explain what every tensor shape means.

## Prerequisites

- [Classical NLP and Seq2Seq](/ai/classical-deep-learning/classical-nlp) for the encoder-decoder structure and the fixed-vector bottleneck this phase replaces
- [Recurrent Networks](/ai/classical-deep-learning/recurrent-networks) for what `O(1)` streaming memory costs you and why giving it up was worth it
- [Normalization](/ai/neural-networks/normalization) for RMSNorm and the pre-norm placement every modern block uses
- [Neurons and Activations](/ai/neural-networks/neurons-and-activations) for GELU and SwiGLU, which the feed-forward blocks use
- [Softmax and Logits](/ai/math-foundations/softmax-and-logits) for the log-sum-exp trick and softmax saturation, which is what the `1 / sqrt(d_k)` scaling prevents
- [Matrix Multiplication](/ai/pytorch/matrix-multiplication) for `matmul` broadcasting and `einsum`, the two ways to write attention

No new packages. Everything here runs on the PyTorch from Phase 2.

---

## What you should be able to do afterwards

- Write `softmax(Q K^T / sqrt(d_k)) V` from memory and state every tensor shape in it
- Derive why the scale factor is `1 / sqrt(d_k)` and not `1 / d_k`, starting from the variance of a dot product of unit-variance vectors
- Explain what a causal mask does, why `-inf` and not `0`, and how masking is what allows a whole sequence to train in one forward pass
- Split attention into heads, say what `d_head = d_model / n_heads` buys, and explain why multiple subspaces beat one wide head
- State what a feed-forward block stores and why its parameter count is roughly twice the attention block's
- Compare sinusoidal, learned, relative-bias, ALiBi, and rotary position encodings, and say what each one buys
- Trace a decoder-only forward pass from token IDs to logits with shapes at every step
- Account for every parameter in GPT-2 small and explain why the total is 124,439,808
- Write and train a character-level GPT end to end

## Canonical resources

- [`karpathy/nanoGPT`](https://github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT) — the shortest complete GPT training and finetuning repository, a few hundred lines in `model.py` and a few hundred more in `train.py`. Read `model.py` before writing your own.
- [`harvardnlp/annotated-transformer`](https://github.com/harvardnlp/annotated-transformer) — _Attention Is All You Need_ reproduced line by line in PyTorch, with the original's hyperparameters
- [`amitshekhariitbhu/ai-engineering-interview-questions`](https://github.com/amitshekhariitbhu/ai-engineering-interview-questions) — the LLM fundamentals and transformer sections
- _Attention Is All You Need_ (Vaswani et al., arXiv:1706.03762) — six encoder and six decoder layers, `d_model = 512`, eight heads, `d_ff = 2048`, 27.3 BLEU on WMT 2014 English-to-German
- Andrej Karpathy, _Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out_ — the same material as the last page, built live on a character-level Shakespeare dataset
- _Transformer Feed-Forward Layers Are Key-Value Memories_ (Geva et al., arXiv:2012.14913) — the evidence for treating the FFN as a memory rather than a nonlinear transform
- _RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding_ (Su et al., arXiv:2104.09864) — where RoPE comes from
- _BERT: Pre-training of Deep Bidirectional Transformers_ (Devlin et al., arXiv:1810.04805) — masked language modelling and the encoder-only family
- _Exploring the Limits of Transfer Learning with a Unified Text-to-Text Transformer_ (Raffel et al., arXiv:1910.10683) — T5 and the text-to-text framing
- _Language Models are Few-Shot Learners_ (Brown et al., arXiv:2005.14165) — GPT-3, and the argument for decoder-only at scale
- Outcome School, _Decoding Transformer Architecture_, _Math behind Attention: Q, K, and V_, _Math behind the sqrt(d_k) Scaling Factor_, _Causal Masking in Attention_, _Cross Attention vs Self Attention_, _Skip Connections in Transformers_, _Feed-Forward Networks in LLMs_, and _Encoder vs Decoder in Transformers_
